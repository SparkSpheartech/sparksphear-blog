Our research is organized around the decisions an owner has to make before a new system earns a place in the business.

We are less interested in collecting software names than in explaining fit. A product can be excellent and still be wrong for a particular team, workflow, budget, or growth stage. Every article type below exists to answer one specific buying decision.

## Comparisons

Comparison articles place two or more tools beside one another around a specific decision - not a feature-by-feature scorecard. We look at the use case, setup effort, integrations, reporting, pricing, limitations, and the business size each option fits.

A useful comparison should help you answer:

- Which option fits the way we work today?
- Which limitations will matter six months from now?
- What will the team have to change to use it well?
- What does it cost in year two, not month one - add-ons, usage charges, processing fees, required hardware?

## Software reviews

A review explains what a product does, who it serves, where it fits, and where it may disappoint. We include limitations because a review that only praises a tool is not useful for a buying decision.

When information comes from a vendor, we treat it as a vendor claim and label it as such. When we have first-hand experience or a documented project result, we identify that context explicitly. We do not present marketing copy as independent testing.

## Pricing guides

Pricing changes frequently, especially for software with add-ons, usage charges, processing fees, onboarding fees, or required hardware. Our pricing guides explain what to confirm before signing up:

- Which features sit behind which plan tier
- What per-user, per-transaction, or per-location fees stack on top
- Contract length, cancellation terms, and data export on the way out
- Where a "cheap" plan becomes expensive once the business grows

Always verify current pricing directly with the vendor before making a purchase. Use our figures as research context, not as a substitute for a current quote.

## Automation playbooks

Playbooks describe a workflow problem and the sequence of decisions needed to improve it. They may cover call intake, quoting, scheduling, follow-up, billing, reporting, or system handoffs.

Each playbook follows the same structure: the operating problem, the decisions an owner must make, the handoffs between tools, and the failure modes we have seen in real deployments. The public article explains the approach. Detailed client-specific architecture and implementation plans belong in a private engagement with SPARKSPHEAR.

## How a topic earns a place in the library

We publish research on a question when three things hold:

1. The decision is expensive to get wrong - a real subscription, a workflow change, or staff retraining.
2. The public information available is mostly vendor marketing.
3. We can add something the vendor pages cannot: workflow context, integration realities, or honest limitations.

That is why the library grows slowly. A thin article that restates a vendor's homepage is worse than no article.

## Our standard for useful research

We aim for:

- A clear search question
- A direct answer near the beginning
- Specific criteria instead of vague praise
- Evidence for material claims
- Honest limitations
- Practical next steps
- A clear distinction between an existing product and a custom build
